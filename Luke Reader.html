About this reader

The gospel text is the King James Version. The patristic commentary is quoted from the Catena Aurea on St Luke — the “golden chain” of the Fathers compiled by St Thomas Aquinas — in the Oxford translation of 1843 (public domain), as given in the Dominican House of Studies electronic edition, which modernises the second-person pronouns and omits the marginal references to each Father’s work; nothing is paraphrased. It is restricted here to Fathers venerated in the Orthodox Church, and to Blessed Theophylact of Ohrid, whose commentary on the Gospels is the standard Orthodox reading of them. This volume leans on St Cyril of Alexandria, whose commentary on St Luke survives largely in the Syriac, and on St Ambrose, who wrote the great Latin exposition of this Gospel; the Carolingians, the medieval Glossa, Origen and Eusebius are excluded here as they are in the other readers. The icons and frescoes are public-domain or freely-licensed photographs of Byzantine, Athonite, Serbian, Sicilian-Byzantine and Russian works. Every icon shown for a passage depicts that passage’s own scene, and no image appears under two passages. Orthodox iconography is built on the feast cycle, the miracles and the saints, and it has no scene-icon for most of the parables and teaching passages — those passages are given as plain text rather than illustrated with a general image of Christ that would misrepresent them.
