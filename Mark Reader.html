About this reader

The gospel text is the King James Version. The patristic commentary is quoted verbatim from the Catena Aurea on St Mark — the “golden chain” of the Fathers compiled by St Thomas Aquinas — in Volume II of the Oxford translation (J.G.F. & J. Rivington, 1842; public domain), restricted here to Fathers venerated in the Orthodox Church and to Blessed Theophylact of Ohrid, whose commentary this volume cites more than any other. The volume’s own preface warns that much of what St Thomas received under the name of St Chrysostom is in truth Victor of Antioch’s, and that its commentary under the name of St Jerome is spurious; the Oxford editors marked those passages Pseudo-Chrys. and Pseudo-Jerome, and every one of them is excluded here. The icons and frescoes are, public-domain or freely-licensed photographs of Byzantine, Athonite, Serbian, Sicilian-Byzantine and Russian works. Every icon shown for a passage depicts that passage’s own scene, and no image appears under two passages. Orthodox iconography is built on the feast cycle, the miracles and the saints, and it has no scene-icon for most of the parables and teaching passages — those passages are given as plain text rather than illustrated with a general image of Christ that would misrepresent them.
